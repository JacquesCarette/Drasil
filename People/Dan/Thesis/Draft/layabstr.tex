\prefacesection{Lay Abstract}

Scientific software is more than a program: requirements documents,
equations, designs, and build instructions also describe the system. Separately
edited copies of the same facts can disagree or omit important details. These
inconsistencies can produce incorrect software or results and make the work
difficult for others to understand, verify, or reproduce. This thesis presents
Drasil, which stores scientific and engineering knowledge and uses it to
generate consistent by construction programs, documents, and supporting files. 
Across six examples,
corrections to stored knowledge appeared consistently in regenerated
documentation and code. Encoding this knowledge exposed missing definitions,
conflicting symbols, hidden unit conversions, and unstated assumptions. Shared
knowledge also produced related software without copying common content. The
Drasil approach shifts work from synchronizing many copies to describing
knowledge once, but requires up-front effort and has a learning curve. The case
studies demonstrate feasibility; controlled studies are still needed to measure
productivity and defect reduction.
