\prefacesection{Lay Abstract}

Scientific software includes more than a program: requirements documents,
equations, designs, and build instructions also describe the system. The same 
facts often appear in several places for different readers. When each copy is 
edited separately, they can disagree or omit important details. This thesis 
presents Drasil, a framework that stores scientific and engineering knowledge 
and uses it to generate requirements documents, programs, and supporting files. 
Across six scientific-computing examples, corrections to the stored knowledge 
appeared consistently in regenerated documentation and code. Encoding the 
knowledge also exposed missing definitions, conflicting symbols, hidden unit 
conversions, and unstated assumptions in existing examples. Shared knowledge 
was reused to produce closely related software without copying common content. 
This approach shifts work from synchronizing many copies to carefully 
describing knowledge once, but requires up-front effort and has a learning 
curve. The case studies demonstrate feasibility; controlled studies are still 
needed to measure productivity and defect reduction.
