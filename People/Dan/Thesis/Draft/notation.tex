\prefacesection{Notation, Definitions, and Abbreviations}

\section*{Notation}
\begin{description}[font=\rmfamily\bfseries, leftmargin=3cm, style=nextline]
    \item[$K$, $L$, $M$] Sets used illustratively to denote chunks of related
    knowledge. This set notation supports the conceptual discussion in
    Chapter~\ref{c:process}; it is not a formal model of Drasil's chunk types.
    \item[$p(\cdot)$] A projection function that selects and presents knowledge
    for a particular artifact, audience, or context.
    \item[$p(K) \equiv K$] An identity projection, which presents the full
    knowledge represented by $K$, possibly using different notation or
    language.
    \item[$p(K) \subset K$] A non-identity projection, which intentionally
    omits some details represented by $K$.
    \item[$\textit{input} \rightarrow \textit{process} \rightarrow
    \textit{output}$] The computational pattern that defines the primary class
    of scientific software considered in this thesis.
\end{description}

\section*{Definitions}
\begin{description}[font=\rmfamily\bfseries, leftmargin=3cm, style=nextline]
    \item[Knowledge] Information that has been structured and contextualized
    to be meaningful and useful. It includes facts, concepts, definitions,
    theories, and relationships relevant to a domain or problem.
    \item[Knowledge chunk] A reusable, uniquely identifiable representation of
    related knowledge. Chunks may encode names, definitions, symbols, units,
    expressions, or relationships and may be composed into richer structures.
    \item[Knowledge projection] A context-specific presentation of selected
    knowledge for a particular artifact or audience.
    \item[Recipe] An intermediate specification that selects knowledge and
    defines how it is organized in one or more generated \sfs{}.
    \item[\SF] A portmanteau of ``software'' and ``artifact.'' The term refers
    to any artifact produced during software development that conveys
    knowledge, including documentation, source code, test cases, and build
    instructions.
    \item[Unnecessary redundancy] Multiple independently editable,
    authoritative representations of the same knowledge. Repeated renderings
    derived from one source are not considered unnecessary redundancy.
\end{description}
